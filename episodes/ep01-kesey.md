# Episode 1: "The CIA Paid for Ken Kesey's First Acid Trip"

**Thumbnail text:** THE CIA PAID
**The B-Side angle:** The Sixties didn't start at Woodstock. They started, at least in part, in a government hospital in Menlo Park, where a broke writing student got $75 a session to take drugs for a research programme the government was funding. From there to a Day-Glo school bus, the Grateful Dead, the Hells Angels, a faked suicide, and the man who drove the Beat Generation straight into the hippie one.
**The bet:** Nora bets the destination sign on Kesey's famous bus was spelled wrong. Cal says no serious historian would let that stand.

> **Performance notes for this script.** Lines in square brackets such as `[laughs]`, `[sighs]` and `[whispers]` are acting cues for the voice model, and they aren't spoken. An em dash at the end of a line means the next speaker cuts in. Short backchannel lines ("Mm-hm." "No.") are intentional, because they're what makes two people sound like they're in a room together. Keep them in.

## THE BROCHURE VERSION

**NORA:** Okay, Cal, give me the brochure. What does the average person know about Ken Kesey?

**CAL:** So the brochure version is… honestly, most people know him for one book. *One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest*, 1962. The mental hospital, Nurse Ratched, McMurphy, the Chief…

**NORA:** The Jack Nicholson movie.

**CAL:** The Jack Nicholson movie, which swept the big five Oscars, and which Kesey, by the way, hated. He refused to watch it.

**NORA:** [gasps] No!

**CAL:** He said so for years. He was furious they cut the Chief's narration. And then the brochure says that he became a kind of hippie guru, drove a bus around, threw some, quote, "Acid Tests," and Tom Wolfe wrote a book about it called *The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test*.

**NORA:** And that's it?

**CAL:** That's the brochure. Author, bus, drugs.

**NORA:** Okay. So that's the B-side. Cal, it is *so* much weirder than that.

## THE B-SIDE: ACT ONE, "THE GUINEA PIG"

`[MUSIC: heist-movie bass line]`

**NORA:** So let's go back to Stanford. Late fifties. Kesey's living in this little bohemian enclave called Perry Lane, cheap cottages, writers, grad students, lots of wine—

**CAL:** —lots of venison chili, if I remember right. Kesey was famous for it.

**NORA:** —*venison chili*, okay, I didn't need that detail, but thank you. And his neighbour is a psychology grad student named Vik Lovell.

**CAL:** Mm-hm.

**NORA:** And Vik is the one who tells him, "Hey, the VA hospital is paying people to take experimental drugs."

**CAL:** And the drugs were… it was a whole menu. LSD, psilocybin, mescaline, some others I can't pronounce on a family show.

**NORA:** [laughs] We're not a family show.

**CAL:** We're… we're a *little bit* of a family show.

**NORA:** So he goes in, they dose him, they ask him questions, they write it all down. And here's the part I love. He doesn't just volunteer. He takes a *job* there. Night shift. As an aide on the psych ward.

**CAL:** And this is where the novel comes from. He's sitting up all night with the patients, and he's watching how the ward is run, and he starts writing.

**NORA:** And some of it, he later said, was written while he was on peyote.

**CAL:** That's his claim. He said the voice of the narrator, Chief Bromden, came to him that way. I'd file it under "probably partly true."

**NORA:** The great American novel, brought to you by the federal government.

**CAL:** Okay, so, I have to stop you there, because this is the important part.

**NORA:** [groans] Here we go.

**CAL:** "The CIA gave Ken Kesey LSD" is the version you see everywhere, and the truth is more boring. And more interesting. The government *was* funding psychedelic research at universities and hospitals in that period, a lot of it secretly, through a CIA programme called MKUltra. And some histories link the Menlo Park studies to that money. But at least one Stanford account describes it as Army-sponsored. So the honest version is: it was government money, quite possibly CIA money, and Kesey had no idea who was paying.

**NORA:** But it was the government.

**CAL:** It was the government.

**NORA:** So I'm right.

**CAL:** You're… directionally right.

**NORA:** [laughs] "Directionally right." That's going on my tombstone.

**CAL:** And there's one detail I genuinely love. When *Cuckoo's Nest* came out, Kesey dedicated it to Vik Lovell, the neighbour. And the dedication says, "To Vik Lovell, who told me dragons did not exist, then led me to their lairs."

**NORA:** [softly] Oh, that's good.

**CAL:** It's a really good line.

**NORA:** That's a *really* good line. Okay. So he's got a bestseller, he's got money, he's got—what's the word—

**CAL:** —a following.

**NORA:** A *following*. And then in 1964, he buys a bus.

## THE B-SIDE: ACT TWO, "THE BUS"

**NORA:** Okay. The bus. It's a 1939 International Harvester school bus. Kesey and his friends, who are now calling themselves the Merry Pranksters—

**CAL:** —which is a very sixties name.

**NORA:** It is the *most* sixties name. They kit it out. Bunks, a sound system, speakers on the outside so they can broadcast to the street, a platform on the roof so you can ride up there—

**CAL:** —which is not safe.

**NORA:** Which is *not safe*, thank you, Cal. And they paint it in every Day-Glo colour that exists. And in June 1964 they drive it from California to New York.

**CAL:** Officially it's a book tour. Kesey has a new novel out, *Sometimes a Great Notion*. And they want to see the World's Fair.

**NORA:** And they film the entire thing. Hours and hours of footage. And now, Cal, I need you to tell people who's driving the bus.

**CAL:** [genuinely delighted] Okay. So this is my favourite fact in this whole story. The driver, for most of the trip, is Neal Cassady.

**NORA:** And who's Neal Cassady?

**CAL:** Neal Cassady is "Dean Moriarty."

**NORA:** From…

**CAL:** From *On the Road*! Jack Kerouac's novel. The bible of the Beat Generation. Dean Moriarty, the wild, fast-talking guy who drives across America—that's Cassady. He's the real person Kerouac based him on.

**NORA:** So the guy who drove the *Beats* across America in the forties—

**CAL:** —is now driving the *hippies* across America in 1964.

**NORA:** It's the same guy. It's one guy. He's the handoff. He just—he just changed passengers!

**CAL:** [laughing] He just changed passengers. It's like one generation got out at a rest stop and the next one got on.

**NORA:** Respect to the bus driver. I'm saying it. Respect to the bus driver.

**CAL:** And it gets better, because when they get to New York, there's a party, and Kerouac comes.

**NORA:** Oh no.

**CAL:** And by 1964, Kerouac is… he's not doing great. He's drinking heavily, he's older, he's kind of conservative now, and he walks in and there's this bunch of kids in face paint with an American flag draped over the couch.

**NORA:** [wincing] Oh no.

**CAL:** And the story goes that he picks up the flag, folds it up properly, and sits off to the side. He barely says a word.

**NORA:** That's so sad. That's the dad showing up at the house party.

**CAL:** It's the father of the Beat Generation, meeting his grandchildren, and not liking them very much.

**NORA:** And then they go and see Timothy Leary, right?

**CAL:** They go to Millbrook, which is the estate in upstate New York where Leary and his colleagues were doing their own, uh, much more academic version of all this. Very serious. Very "the sacred journey of the self."

**NORA:** And the Pranksters show up in a Day-Glo bus with smoke bombs.

**CAL:** And it didn't go well. The way it's usually told, Leary didn't even come downstairs to meet them.

**NORA:** [laughs] That's the most East Coast thing that's ever happened.

**CAL:** And Kesey, about the World's Fair, wrote in his journal that it was, quote, "not a cool place."

**NORA:** Okay, the World's Fair had a *robot Abraham Lincoln*, Ken.

**CAL:** That was Disney. That was the Illinois pavilion. That's… that's actually correct, I'm impressed.

**NORA:** I contain multitudes.

## CITATION NEEDED: "THE KOOL-AID"

`[SFX: typewriter ding]`

**CAL:** Okay. Time for "Citation Needed."

**NORA:** [excited] My favourite.

**CAL:** It's *my* segment.

**NORA:** It's my favourite *of* your segments.

**CAL:** [sighs] Thank you. So when the Pranksters get home, they start throwing parties. Kesey calls them the Acid Tests. Late 1965, into '66. Big rented halls, light shows, strobes, tape loops, and a house band who were then called the Grateful Dead.

**NORA:** The Grateful Dead were the *house band*?

**CAL:** The Grateful Dead were the house band. And the LSD at a lot of these events was made by a man named Owsley Stanley, a chemist, who also helped build the Dead's sound system.

**NORA:** So he was making the drugs *and* the speakers.

**CAL:** Vertical integration.

**NORA:** [laughs] Okay, so what's the myth?

**CAL:** The myth is the title of the book. *The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test*. The idea that people were walking up to a bowl of punch, having a cup, and finding out the hard way.

**NORA:** Was that true?

**CAL:** IT'S COMPLICATED. At most of the Acid Tests, people knew exactly what they were there for. That was the point. But at one of them, in Los Angeles, in Watts, in early 1966, there was a big container of Kool-Aid that was spiked, and not everybody knew.

**NORA:** Oh, that's bad.

**CAL:** That's bad. People had a very rough night.

**NORA:** So the most famous phrase in the whole story comes from the one night it went wrong.

**CAL:** Pretty much. And keep that phrase in your head, because it's going to come back in this show. In a much, much darker episode.

**NORA:** [quietly] Yeah. Yeah, it is.

**NORA:** He fakes his own *death*! He has a friend drive his truck up the coast and leave it at the edge of a cliff, near the ocean, with a suicide note.

**CAL:** And by then LSD is illegal in California. That happened in October 1966. The Acid Tests are over. And honestly, the moment is over too.

**NORA:** So the government pays for his first trip, and then the government makes it illegal.

**CAL:** Full circle.

**NORA:** Full. Circle.
